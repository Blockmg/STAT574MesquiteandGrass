# Group project: Description
STAT 574E

Overview
The group project is an opportunity to demonstrate your command of the statistical methods presented in this class through an application of linear models to data of your choice. As a group, you will select one or more data sets to analyze, identify questions of interest and a plan to answer them, execute that plan (possibly updating the plan where necessary), and finally you will share your findings in the form of an oral presentation and written report.

Roles
Your group should designate the following team roles. Feel free to modify these roles as long as the various duties are balanced around the group. If you have fewer than four group members, make sure you still cover all roles.

Initiator + Delegator: spearheads distribution of labor, propels project forward
Harmonizer + Communicator: identifies conflict and helps the group move past it, point person for communication with Henry
Timekeeper + Scheduler: keeps track and reminds the group of key deadlines, identifies times for group meetings
Deliverables Manager: verifies compliance with assignment instructions throughout project process and checks final documents for consistency
Once your group has selected roles for each member, have the appropriate person send an email with roles to Henry with each group member CC-ed.

Exploration [about 1-3 pages, submitted independently]
[6/60 pts]

Each person will come up with a possible direction for the group project independently. This exploration of an idea for the project does not need to be fully developed, but it should address the following:

Possible topic: What is the topic? Why would it be interesting? What questions could you investigate? Why is it a good fit for the timing/size of a class project?
Identify a potential data set to work with: Do some basic exploratory data analysis to establish that a potential data set will work for your project. Where did you find the data? Why did you choose it? What are some interesting features of the data? Provide figures, tables, and other summaries that help explain these features.
Possible statistical model/methods: What model(s)/method(s) would make sense for the data and research questions? What tools/packages would you use for implementation?
Possible challenges: What challenges might come up? If possible, suggest possible ways to address challenges.

Proposal [about 2-3 pages, one submission per group]
[6/60 pts]

As a group, you will discuss each the explorations developed by each member and agree on a topic to pursue going forward. It can be one of the explorations, a blend of more than one, or even something new. Once you decide on a topic, the next step is to identify interesting questions you hope to answer through a careful analysis. Your questions should be clear, specific, and relevant. You will answer your questions by examining the data and interpreting the fits of appropriate statistical models. Examples of good primary research questions are:

Is there a systematic difference in outcomes on the SAT between men and women? If so, what factors are relevant in predicting that difference?
What qualities of burrito/taco restaurants best predict their review scores on Yelp? What are the relationships between these factors and review score?
How does the probability of a flight being cancelled or delayed depend on the following variables…
Your group will submit a proposal document to D2L that details three primary research questions you plan to answer and what statistical methods you will be use to answer them. The document will also include a description of what each member of your group will do to contribute. The document should address the following questions:

What three primary research questions do you plan to answer?
What statistical methods do you plan to use to answer those questions? For each method, explain why it is appropriate for your data and how it will help to answer at least one of your research questions.
What will each person in the group do to contribute? When will each task be completed? Provide a detailed description of the responsibilities for each group member. Often, students find a table a good way organize tasks and planned deadlines. Note: If these responsibilities seem out of balance, I reserve the right to ask you to adjust them.
What complications/challenges do you expect to encounter? How might you address them?
Checking in…
Your group will schedule a brief (10-20min) check-in with Henry mid-way through the project (see Calendar for specific dates). The check-in can be in-person or virtual. During this meeting, you and Henry will discuss your proposal. Henry will let you know if there is anything concerning about your proposed plan of action and try to point out potential future challenges that he thinks might arise. You should come prepared to relate how work is progressing so far and ask questions about issues/challenges that have come up.

Presentation [15–20 minute, one submission per group]
Final week of classes [15/60 pts]

Your group will create a 15–20 minute presentation in which you will introduce your topic and data, describe your research goals, explain the methods you used, and summarize your findings. Make sure to address each of your research questions, including how you answered it and/or what challenges prevented you from fully answering it. You can give your presentation live in class, or prepare a pre-recorded video, whichever your group prefers (send recordings to me 24hrs in advance if you opt for a pre-recorded video). A rubric for how this presentation will be assessed will be provided in advance.

Presentation participation [individual]
Final week of classes [6/60 pts]

During each presentation non-presenting groups will participate by listening attentively, asking questions, and preparing feedback addressing the following points:

What part(s) of the presentation resonated with you most? Which part was hardest to follow?
What are one or two follow-up questions you have for the group?
Credit for participation requires attendance. Online students, please get in touch with me if you will be unable to attend class during presentations.

Report [5-7 pages, one submission per group]
Finals Week [27/60 pts]

Your group will prepare a written document that details the material presented in your talk. The document should follow the traditional structure of a statistical report:

Title + Authors: Please provide a meaningful title for your report and all authors’ names.
Executive Summary (like an abstract): One paragraph that describes the questions, how they were addressed, results, and conclusions.
Introduction: What is/are the question(s)? What type of statistical analyses are used and why is it appropriate?
Exploratory Data Analysis: Describe the data with the help of appropriate tables and figures.
Statistical Analyses: These should be described to someone who is a quantitatively savvy scientist, not to your statistics professor. Address any model assumptions and check that there are no extreme violations. Interpret results in plain language.
Conclusions: Summarize results what what real-world implications they have (e.g., are there any new/changes to existing policies a business/organization might consider based on your findings?). Discuss limitations of the study. Discuss possible future studies.
References: Cite relevant resources and include a bibliography (use a consistent style such as APA: https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/index.html).
Appendix (separate file): Include additional figures, peripheral text not suitable for the sections outlined above, annotated computer code (this is the only place you should include large blocks of code), and other additional materials. Someone else should be able to run your code to get to the results you presented in the paper. Important results should be included in the paper, not in the appendix. Please submit code as a separate .R or .Rmd file.
Note: the due date for the written report is later than the presentation date to provide as much flexibility for your group as possible during the busy finals period of the semester. Some groups may find it helpful to prepare their report first, then create their presentation.
